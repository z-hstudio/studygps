# Alex: one-minute application demo

Open the English public demo at `/demo.html?lang=en`. Keep the teacher workspace ready at `/portal.html?lang=en&demo=alex#student-detail`. The teacher link selects Alex only from the signed-in teacher's authorised synthetic classroom roster. Public controls do not update classroom records.

## Speaker 2

Let’s look at Alex’s study route. Each task shows what to study, when to start, how to practise, and when to review.

Move his assignment deadline closer, and the priorities change: same grades, new deadline, new priority.

Now, the teacher’s view. Teachers can see their own students’ goals, grades, and progress, then give individual feedback.

We also demonstrate how sleep can affect a study schedule using simulated data.

As deadlines, results, and progress change, StudyGPS updates the route.

Students know what to do next. Teachers can see where to help.

Now, how does this work behind the scenes?

## Screen cues

- 0–25 seconds: public demo, Alex first assessment. Change the second-law assignment to “Due in 2 days”; wait for “Route updated” before describing the new priority.
- 25–45 seconds: switch to the prepared teacher tab. Show Alex's goal, assessment, route and teacher guidance. These are a separate classroom example, not a synchronised result of changing the public demo.
- 45–55 seconds: briefly show the health simulation and deliver the final two lines; leave five seconds for pauses. Alex's classroom example shows a rested 7.8-hour night, which leaves the schedule unchanged. The public “Short sleep” scenario demonstrates reduced study time. Both are fictional; blood oxygen does not affect the plan.
- Rehearse with a timer. Do not type feedback, sign in, play a full audio clip or read every task during the one-minute slot.

## Language behaviour

English and Chinese select authored translations for synthetic names, goals, advice, planning context and task explanations. Existing stored records, custom user prose, scores and completion are preserved. The language selector itself intentionally names both languages. A locale query never grants classroom access.
